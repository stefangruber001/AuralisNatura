01 — Your starting point

Where you are today

▼ Reflect their story back warmly, in their own words — so they feel truly heard.

You came to Auralis feeling persistently tired — the kind of tiredness that sleep doesn't seem to fix — and noticing that your energy, mood and digestion have all shifted since your daughter was born. You're doing everything you can think of, and you'd like a clearer, calmer path forward that fits a busy life.

This report gathers what you shared and turns it into a few honest observations and a realistic first plan. It is education and guidance for your wellbeing — a companion to, never a replacement for, your doctor's care.

02 — What we're seeing

The themes that stand out

▼ Name the key patterns simply & honestly. Educational — never a diagnosis.
Theme 01

Energy & recovery

Several small things — irregular meals, broken sleep, high demands — are likely compounding into the fatigue you're feeling.

Theme 02

Nourishment rhythm

Long gaps between meals and low protein at breakfast may be affecting your energy and cravings through the day.

Theme 03

Stress & nervous system

This life stage asks a lot of your nervous system; small daily moments of recovery can make a real difference.

03 — The science, simply

The "why" behind the plan

▼ Explain the reasoning in clear, credible terms. Say plainly where evidence is mixed.

When meals are spread out and low in protein, blood sugar tends to swing — which can show up as energy dips, cravings and low mood. Starting the day with a protein- and fibre-rich breakfast helps steady that curve, which is why it's the first small change we'll focus on.

Sleep and stress are deeply linked to how we feel and eat. You won't be able to control broken nights right now, but protecting even short, consistent moments of rest helps your nervous system recover — and the research here is genuinely strong.

In one sentence

Small, steady, realistic changes — not a dramatic overhaul — are what will move the needle for you right now.

04 — Your plan

Where to begin — in order

▼ Two or three prioritised, realistic actions. Sequenced, never overwhelming.
Step 01 · This week

A steadier breakfast

Aim for a breakfast with protein and fibre within an hour of waking — eggs, yoghurt, oats with seeds. One change, repeated daily.

Step 02 · This week

Ten minutes of rest

One small, protected pause each day — a short walk, breathing, or quiet. Consistency matters more than length.

Step 03 · Next 2–3 weeks

Even meal spacing

Gently close the longest gaps between meals so your energy has fewer dips. We'll refine this together as we go.

Step 04 · Ongoing

Notice & adjust

Keep a light note of energy and mood. We'll use what you notice to shape the next steps — this is a partnership, not a prescription.

05 — When to see a doctor

Your safety comes first

▼ Always include this. Be clear & caring about anything that warrants medical attention.
Please check in with your GP

Because persistent fatigue can sometimes have an underlying medical cause, I'd warmly encourage you to ask your doctor about a routine check (for example, iron, thyroid and vitamin D) — especially postpartum. Nothing in this report diagnoses or treats any condition, and if anything ever feels acute or worrying, please contact your doctor or, in an emergency, call 112. This guidance supports your medical care; it never replaces it.
