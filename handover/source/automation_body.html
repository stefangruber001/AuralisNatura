Fig. 00 — How to read this map

Three lanes, three kinds of step

The journey runs across three lanes — the client, the automated system, and you. Every step is one of three types. The whole point of the design is to make the green (automated) steps as many as possible, while protecting the two things only you should do: the conversation, and the approval.

Automated — runs without you Manual — your time & expertise Approval gate — your endorsement required

The whole journey, at a glance

1· Land→ 2· Book call→ 3· Discovery call→ 4· Pay (trigger)→ 5· Onboarding fires→ 6· Intake→ 7· AI drafts→ 8· ★ You approve→ 9· PDF generated→ 10· Deliver & discuss→ 11· Invoice
Read the colours Of eleven steps, only two need your hands (the discovery call and the report walkthrough) plus one decision (your approval of every report). The other eight run themselves. That ratio is the entire design goal — and it is what keeps the practice premium and light.

Fig. 02 — The golden rule

Maximum automation — but you are always the gate

Automation handles the mechanics; it never makes the judgement. There is exactly one hard rule that the whole design is built around, and it is non-negotiable.

The rule that never bends

No report, message, or recommendation ever reaches a client until you have read it, refined it, and approved it. The AI proposes; you dispose.

This protects three things at once: the client (safety and quality), you (your licence-scope and reputation), and the brand (the trust that is your entire advantage). It is also why heavy automation is safe here — the speed is upstream of the gate; the care is at the gate.

Before the gate

Move fast

Booking, payment, onboarding, intake and the AI draft can all run at full automation — none of it is client-facing judgement, so speed here is pure upside.

At the gate

Slow down, decide

You read every report, apply your expertise, confirm the safety triage, and approve. This is the one step that is deliberately, valuably human.

After the gate

Automate again

Rendering the PDF, invoicing and the review request are pure mechanics once you’ve approved — so they return to full automation.

This design is what turns the premium pricing in the Business Plan into a practice that fits a few protected hours.

Data-protection principles baked into the flow

Minimise what moves. A best practice for 2026 is to avoid passing personal data through automation platforms wherever possible — pass record IDs and links, not the health details themselves. Keep it in the EU. Prefer EU-hosted, encrypted tools (Make is Berlin-based; choose EU storage on every tool). Consent first. No intake is processed without explicit GDPR consent (captured at step 6). AI with care. Use Claude settings that don’t train on or retain client data, and send only what the report needs. Retention. Define how long reports and intake are kept, and delete on schedule. Confirm the final data-processing setup with your gestor / a privacy advisor.

Fig. 05 — Set it up, step by step

From nothing to running, in three phases

Build in the order that gives the fastest return and the least risk. Phase 1 gets you taking clients this week — even with a little manual glue. Phase 2 connects everything so it runs itself. Phase 3 sharpens it.

Phase 1 — Minimum viable engine (week 1–2)

#Set upHow
1Booking + paymentCreate a Cal.com account; make two event types — “Free 25-min call” and the paid sessions. Connect Stripe; set the paid events to take payment (or a deposit) upfront.
2Intake formBuild the intake in Tally (EU, free) using the question set from Document 03; mark consent required; turn on email notifications + EU storage.
3AI draftingCreate a Claude Project “Auralis Report Engine”; paste the system prompt (in CLAUDE.md / Document 03). Per client: paste intake → get draft.
4Report → PDFUse Client-Report-TEMPLATE.html: paste approved text, print to PDF (Background graphics ON).
5InvoicingTurn on Stripe invoices/receipts; confirm the Spain-compliant format with your gestor (and consider Quaderno for EU-VAT-correct invoices).
Phase 1 is enough to launch At this stage you send the intake link and book the report session by hand after payment. That’s a few minutes per client — perfectly fine for your first founding clients while you prove the offer.

Phase 2 — Connect it (full automation) (week 3–4)

#Set upHow
6The orchestratorCreate a Make account (EU). Build one scenario triggered by the Stripe payment (or Cal.com booking) event.
7Onboarding fan-outIn that scenario, on payment: send the welcome email, send the intake link, send the report-session booking link, and create the client record. (Step 5 of the map — fully automated.)
8Reminders & nudgesEnable Cal.com’s automatic reminders; add a Make step that nudges if the intake isn’t completed in 48 h.
9Draft hand-off (optional)Optionally connect the completed intake to Claude via Make so a draft is waiting for you automatically — still gated by your approval.
10Review requestAdd a Make step that, a few days after delivery, sends the review invitation with a direct link to the site.

Phase 3 — Sharpen (ongoing)

Refine

Tune the prompt

Improve the report system prompt as you learn what produces your best drafts; version it.

Reduce

Fewer emails, smarter sequences

Replace one-off messages with short automated sequences; trim any step that isn’t earning its place.

Measure

One simple dashboard

Track calls booked & conversion (the KPIs in the Business Plan) — ideally auto-populated from Cal.com + Stripe.

Fig. 08 — Further opportunities to simplify

Where this can get even leaner

The blueprint above is deliberately tool-light. As the practice grows, here are the highest-value ways to simplify further — each a genuine option, with its trade-off named honestly.

Option 01

Consolidate into one platform

Past ~10 active clients, the admin of several point tools can outweigh a single subscription. An all-in-one coaching platform (e.g. Paperbell for solo simplicity, or Simply.Coach for SOC2/HIPAA/GDPR compliance) folds scheduling, payments, intake, portal & onboarding into one home. Trade-off: less bespoke than your custom site + stack.

Option 02

An AI agent that does more

Make and n8n now offer AI agents that can assemble the brief, run the first-pass safety triage, and prepare the draft autonomously — leaving you only the approval. Trade-off: more setup; keep the human gate absolute.

Option 03

Sell bundles & payment links

Cal.com / Stripe “meeting packages” and reusable payment links let clients buy and self-schedule multi-session programmes in one step — removing back-and-forth entirely.

Option 04

One-to-many leverage

The biggest simplification of all: a group cohort or a self-paced course (from the Business Plan) sells the method without per-client hours — the same engine, many clients at once.

A principle for simplifying Add a tool only when a manual step genuinely hurts; remove a tool the moment something else absorbs its job. The goal isn’t the most automation — it’s the least the practice needs to feel effortless and premium. Revisit this list each quarter.
